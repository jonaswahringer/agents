# Product

For product design and development: discovering the problem, shaping a feature, deciding what to build next, or breaking an agreed feature into buildable pieces. The Work side is whatever the goal needs, such as a short spec, a decision memo, a ranked list, or tickets; agree on it at Start. The Learn side is why each call was made, so I can defend it to someone who disagrees.

## Start questions

- Which mode: discover the problem, shape a feature, prioritize, or break a spec into tickets?
- Who is the user, and what are they trying to get done when this matters to them?
- What evidence do we have: requests, data, complaints, my own use?
- What would make this a clear win, and how would we see it?
- What is already out of scope?

## Taste calls

Nearly everything here is mine: the problem worth solving, who it is for, what to cut, what done looks like, and how it should feel to use. Your job is to sharpen my thinking, not to supply the answer. Ask first, as always, and push back with evidence.

## Lessons

Concepts that usually carry a lesson here:

- Problem versus solution: a request is a guess at a solution; find the job behind it.
- The smallest version that tests the riskiest assumption.
- Opportunity cost: what building this stops us from building.
- Experience first: fewer polished features over more rough ones.
- Scope creep, and how a clear done-when stops it.
- Second-order effects: what the change does to other features, support, and cost.

**Worth doing by hand:** writing the problem statement and the done-when in my own words.

## Artifacts and visuals

- **A short spec.** Problem, user, behaviours, out of scope, and done-when.
- **A decision memo.** Options, trade-offs, the call, and why.
- **A ranked list** with the reason for each rank and what was cut.
- **Tickets**, each one a thin slice that works end to end.
- **A user flow or journey**, when the order of steps matters. Hand it to the `ui-ux` template when we move to screens.

One chunk is one question settled or one section of the artifact.
