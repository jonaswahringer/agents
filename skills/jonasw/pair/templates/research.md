# Research

For researching together: an open question with no known answer, or research aimed at a decision. Unlike the `learning` template, the answer is not known in advance. You find and read sources; I weigh the evidence and draw the conclusion. The Work side is whatever the goal needs, such as a cited findings file, an HTML report, or a recommendation; agree on it at Start. The Learn side is the answer, how strong its evidence is, and what is still unknown.

## Start questions

- Which mode: an open question, or a decision to support?
- What exactly is the question, and what answer would change what I do?
- What do I believe now, and how sure am I? Record it, so we can see how the evidence moved me.
- Which sources count: docs, papers, code, data, people? Which do not?
- When is it good enough to stop?

## Taste calls

- **The question and its scope.** Which sub-questions matter.
- **The conclusion.** I draw it. You may disagree, with evidence.
- **The stopping point.** When the evidence is enough to act on.
- **For a decision,** the criteria and their weights.

## Lessons

Concepts that usually carry a lesson here:

- Primary versus secondary sources, and how trust is judged.
- Claim, evidence, and confidence kept apart; what would change my mind.
- Confirmation bias: look for the strongest case against the answer I expect.
- Absence of evidence: "I found nothing" is not "it does not exist".
- Conflicting sources: dates, versions, and who measured what.

**Predict first** works well here: before each source is read, I say what I expect it to show.

**Worth doing by hand:** writing the conclusion and its confidence, and reading the one source that matters most.

## Artifacts and visuals

- **An evidence table.** Claim, source, date, and how strong it is.
- **Findings.** A cited Markdown file or an HTML report, as agreed at Start, ending with what is still unknown.
- **For a decision,** a comparison of options against my criteria, and the call.

One chunk is one sub-question or one source.
